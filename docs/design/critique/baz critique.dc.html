11b The overview — zoom out until the whole collection fits

The radical end of the same idea: zoom out until there is nothing to scroll. At 24px a 1,200-album collection fits one screen; the amber halo still finds the playing record from orbit. No captions — at this altitude the wall is a map, and you navigate it the way you spot a spine across the room: by color and position. Click lands you on that shelf at full size. This is “your collection IS the interface” at its purest, and virtualization makes it nearly free. Zoom becomes the density setting from 1b — continuous, spatial, no settings menu.
Together: the wall stops being a feed. Landmarks (11a) + altitude (11b) + filter (4a) mean scroll is a stroll of a few shelves at most, never a commute. One iced caveat: smooth zoom wants the animation runtime you don't have — ship it as 3–4 discrete zoom stops (full / half / quarter / orbit), which is honestly more shelf-like anyway.

10b Shuffle what you can see — the constraint is the wall itself
GENRE · DUB · 14
shuffling this shelf · Halide — Moss & Wire · next two marked
One mental model kills the whole “smart shuffle” feature tree: shuffle always plays from whatever the wall currently shows. Grouped by genre and standing on the DUB shelf? Shuffle dubs. Typed “kra” (4a)? Shuffle the matches. Whole wall? Shuffle everything. Vibe-shuffle needs no new UI, no ML, no mode — the filter IS the vibe, and it's always visible: non-pool covers dim to 35%, the next two draws carry faint rings, so shuffle stops being a dice roll behind a curtain. It composes with the stack (6a) and the pull (6b) without touching either.
On “pedestrian”: play-album and play-song should stay boring — any invention there is a tax before sound, and the friction budget (4b) forbids it. Spend the strangeness where the category is lazy: shuffle. If you later want true vibe-shuffle (tempo, energy, era-adjacency from audio analysis), it slots in as one more group key — SHELF · MOOD — not as a new feature. What I'd still refuse: any shuffle that draws from records the wall isn't showing; the moment the pool is invisible, you've rebuilt the curtain.

The discipline: a theme sets exactly two values — room temperature and room lightness. Ink takes the opposite temperature (that split IS the brand, so it survives every theme), the accent clamp-arc flips to oppose the room, and the elevation scale keeps its L-deltas verbatim (light theme rises darker instead of lighter). What I'd refuse: user-picked accent colors — the accent means “playback truth,” and a preference picker dilutes it into decoration.
